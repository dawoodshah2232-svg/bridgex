Funnels

A funnel is a question, not a decoration.

Where do leads stall? Where do applications drop off? Where does funding fail? A funnel that cannot be drilled into — down to the actual records at each stage — is wallpaper. BridgeX funnels open into the queues behind them.

Sales funnel — lead to funded account

Each stage is a live count of real records. Click a stage and you see the leads in it, their desks, and how long they have waited.

Partner funnel — referral to payout

Referred clients, attributed signups, earned commissions and settled payouts — the partner's journey and the business's, in one view.

Onboarding funnel — application to approval

Documents complete, checks passed, approvals granted — with drop-off visible by country, client type and step, so the fix lands on the step, not the applicant.

Decisions are made with a view of the whole business — not one desk's spreadsheet.

Exceptions

The platform tells you what needs a human.

Most of the business runs without attention. Exceptions are the small part that should not — items that did not match, complete or reconcile, each carrying the records and the reason it was raised.

Finance

A payment that did not reconcile

The provider says settled; the ledger says otherwise. The exception carries the request, the provider event and the ledger entry — so finance investigates one break, not a month.

Compliance

A document about to expire

An identity document lapses in thirty days. The exception exists before it becomes a blocked account — and the client has already been asked for a replacement.

Compliance

A screening flag awaiting review

A watchlist screen returned a possible match. The exception holds the flag, the evidence and the application — and waits for a human decision, not an automatic one.

Sales

A lead gone quiet

Seven days without activity on a qualified lead. The exception is a nudge, not an alarm — but it is visible, assigned and ageing in the open.

Exceptions are ordered by risk and age, assigned to a role, and tracked until resolved. An exception that nobody owns is not an exception queue — it is a pile. BridgeX keeps the queue owned.

Payment monitoring

Watch the money move — and watch it match.

Deposits, withdrawals, provider performance and reconciliation state, monitored continuously. The question is never 'did the money move' — it is 'does every record agree that it moved'.

Provider health at a glance

Approval rates, decline reasons and settlement timing per provider — so a degrading provider is visible before clients start reporting failures.

Declines with a reason, not a code

A declined payment shows its reason in plain language, with the retry path already decided by the routing rules — cascading retry is a configuration, not a support task.

Settlement you can prove

Every payment traces from request to provider event to ledger entry. When finance closes the day, the trail is the evidence — not a reconstruction from three dashboards.

Routing and retry detail lives on the payment orchestration page.

Exports and audit trails

Reports and dashboards export in standard formats for your own analysis — and exports are themselves logged, so data leaving the platform is accounted for. The audit trail behind them records who did what, when, and what changed, in a form a reviewer can follow without a developer in the room.

  • Scheduled and on-demand exports, per role
  • Exports logged: who exported what, and when
  • Audit events searchable by record, actor and date
  • Before-and-after values on significant changes
