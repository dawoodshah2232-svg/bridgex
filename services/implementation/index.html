Services — Implementation

Five phases. No surprises.

Implementation is a joint project with a named team on both sides, a written plan, and a gate at the end of every phase. You always know where things stand — and nothing goes live until your team has tested it.

The method

Discover, design, build, validate, deploy.

Each phase ends with an exit gate: a decision, in writing, that the work is done well enough to proceed. Select a phase to see what happens in it, what it produces, and what closes it.

We learn how your business actually runs — desks, products, regions, approval habits — before anything is configured.

Joint delivery

Who owns what — agreed up front.

Most implementation problems are ownership problems in disguise. This matrix is agreed before work starts, so nobody discovers mid-project that a task belonged to no one.

For each implementation area, what BridgeX owns and what the client owns.
AreaBridgeX ownsYou own
Discovery & designRun workshops, draft the design, challenge assumptions.Provide people, decisions and access to current systems.
ConfigurationBuild and document the configuration in staging.Review and approve each configured area.
IntegrationsBuild and test adapters against agreed specifications.Supply credentials, sandbox access and a test contact.
Data migrationMap, migrate and reconcile data in stages.Own the source data and confirm its quality.
ValidationSupport testing and fix what testing finds.Run acceptance tests and sign off — or say what fails.
TrainingDeliver role-based training and reference material.Make the right people available, at the right time.
Go-liveExecute the cutover runbook and hypercare.Approve the go-live decision and the rollback trigger.

The pattern is deliberate: BridgeX does the building, your team does the deciding. We will never ask you to configure the system yourself, and we will never make a business decision on your behalf.

Deliverables

What you actually receive.

No vague promises. These are the artefacts every implementation produces, and they stay with you whether or not you continue with managed services.

  • Discovery & scope statement

    What we agreed to build, and what success looks like.

  • Configuration design

    Data model, journeys, roles and integrations — documented.

  • Staging environment

    The full build, tested by your team before it matters.

  • Reconciliation report

    Migrated data proven against the source, in writing.

  • Training records

    Who was trained, on what, and what they can do now.

  • Go-live runbook & handover

    Cutover steps, rollback trigger, and the support handover.
