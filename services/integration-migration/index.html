Services — Integration & Migration

Your history moves with you. Verified, not hoped for.

Migration is where most platform projects fail — quietly, in the data. We map every source, migrate in stages to a staging environment first, reconcile against the source, and agree a rollback plan before anything goes live.

Before anything moves

The discovery checklist.

Every migration starts with these questions. If you can answer most of them, you are further along than most. If you cannot, that is what discovery is for — we work through them with you.

  • 01Which systems hold client, account and transaction data today?
  • 02Who owns each source system, and who can authorise access?
  • 03What data quality issues already exist — duplicates, gaps, stale records?
  • 04Which historical data must move, and which can be archived?
  • 05What regulatory retention rules apply to each data type?
  • 06Which integrations must keep running during the migration?
  • 07Who signs off that the migrated data is correct?

The migration path

Five stages, each one proven before the next.

Staging first, production last. Reconciliation closes the loop — and sign-off only happens when the numbers agree or the gap is explained.

  1. 1

    01Discovery

    We inventory every source system, file and feed — and agree what moves, what stays, and what gets archived.

    • Source systems and owners identified
    • Data volumes, formats and quality assessed
    • Retention and privacy requirements noted
  2. 2

    02Source mapping

    Every source field is mapped to its BridgeX destination, with rules for transformation, defaults and duplicates.

    • Field-by-field mapping document
    • Duplicate and conflict rules agreed
    • Sensitive data handling defined
  3. 3

    03Staging migration

    Data is migrated into a staging environment first, so your team can inspect the result before it matters.

    • Full migration rehearsed in staging
    • Record counts and samples reviewed
    • Issues fixed and re-run, not patched live
  4. 4

    04Dual-write & cutover

    Where it applies, both systems run in parallel for a defined window — then cutover follows a written runbook.

    • Dual-write agreed where technically feasible
    • Cutover runbook and rollback trigger written
    • Freeze window communicated to all teams
  5. 5

    05Reconciliation

    After cutover, totals and samples are reconciled against the source until the numbers agree — or the gap is explained.

    • Record and balance totals compared
    • Exceptions listed and resolved
    • Sign-off only when reconciliation closes

Safety nets

What protects the cutover.

Three mechanisms, described honestly — including what each one can and cannot do.

Dual-write, where applicable

Where the systems support it, both old and new run in parallel for a defined window, so a problem in one does not strand you. Where dual-write is not technically feasible, we say so in the plan — we do not pretend otherwise.

Rollback, agreed in advance

The rollback plan — including the trigger that activates it — is written before cutover, not improvised during it. What rollback can cover depends on the systems involved, and the runbook is explicit about that.

Reconciliation, not trust

After cutover, record counts, balances and samples are compared against the source. Every exception is listed and resolved — or explicitly explained — before the migration is accepted.
