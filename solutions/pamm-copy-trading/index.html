Capabilities

The whole lifecycle, manager to investor

Discovery, subscription, allocation, fees and settlement — each step defined, logged and visible to the people it affects.

Discovery & subscription

Investors find strategies they understand, and join them deliberately.

  • Strategy discovery

    A catalogue of manager strategies with performance history, risk profile and participation terms.

  • Investor subscriptions

    Subscribe with an allocation amount, minimum and maximum participation, and clear terms.

  • Performance dashboard

    Strategy and investor views of returns, drawdown and fees — the same underlying figures, both sides.

  • Copy-mode risk settings

    Followers set their own limits: proportional or fixed sizing, maximum exposure and stop conditions.

Fees & allocation

Fee logic that can be shown, not just stated.

  • High-water-mark fees

    Performance fees charged only on new profit above the previous peak — the watermark is visible, not hidden.

  • Performance & management fees

    Both fee types modelled, scheduled and reported per investor and per period.

  • Investor allocation

    Capital allocated across strategies with per-investor balances and share tracking.

  • Unsubscribe & settlement

    A defined exit: positions settled, fees calculated to the exit date, balance returned with a statement.

Execution & oversight

Keeping follower accounts in step with the strategy — and proving it.

  • Position synchronisation

    Follower positions mirror the strategy through the platform adapter, with sync state visible.

  • Rounding & drift monitoring

    Rounding differences and drift from the master strategy are measured and reported, not ignored.

  • Netting vs hedging

    Position handling follows the account model — netting or hedging — as configured for each setup.

  • Audit & transparency

    Subscriptions, allocations, fees and settlements all logged, with investor-facing statements.

The problem

Managed strategies run on trust — and trust needs evidence

Investors follow a strategy because they believe in it. But when fees, allocations and performance live in separate spreadsheets, belief is all they have — and the first dispute costs the relationship.

  1. 01Fees nobody can verifyPerformance fees calculated off-platform leave investors taking the number on faith.
  2. 02Allocation by handInvestor shares worked out in spreadsheets, with rounding differences nobody can explain.
  3. 03Risk settings that driftA follower's limits set once and never revisited, while the strategy and the market both move on.
  4. 04No clear exitUnsubscribing means emails, manual settlement and arguments about the final balance.

How it flows

From discovery to settlement

An investor's journey through a strategy, with the fee and the exit defined before the first trade is copied.

  1. 1

    Discover

    The investor browses strategies, compares performance and risk, and reads the terms.

  2. 2

    Subscribe

    An allocation is committed, with participation limits and copy-mode risk settings.

  3. 3

    Allocate

    Capital is allocated and positions begin synchronising with the strategy.

  4. 4

    Follow

    Trades mirror to the investor account; drift and rounding are monitored continuously.

  5. 5

    Settle fees

    Fees are calculated against the high-water mark and reported per period.

  6. 6

    Exit

    Unsubscribe settles positions and fees, and returns the balance with a statement.

Why it matters

Trust, built on arithmetic

  • Benefit 1: Fees investors can check

    High-water-mark logic shown step by step, so a fee is a calculation — not a claim.

  • Benefit 2: Risk settings that belong to the investor

    Followers set their own limits instead of inheriting the manager's appetite wholesale.

  • Benefit 3: Allocation without the spreadsheet

  • Benefit 4: Drift you can see

Security & permissions

Controls around other people's money

Copy trading moves real positions on real accounts. Limits, approvals and audit are part of the design — not added later.

Control by design

  • Investor risk limits

    Per-investor exposure caps, sizing rules and stop conditions enforced at the follower level.

  • Separation of roles

    Managers manage strategies; they never touch investor accounts or fee configuration.

  • Fee audit trail

    Every fee calculation keeps its inputs — watermark, period, rate — so it can be re-derived.

  • Settlement records

    Subscriptions, exits and settlements logged with balances and statements.

Integration approach

Connected, not isolated

The trading platform is where positions live; BridgeX is where the relationship, the allocation and the fee live. Adapters connect the two.

  • Trading platforms

    Position synchronisation through platform adapters, per account model — netting or hedging.

  • Financial CRM

    Investor and manager records, subscriptions and suitability held on the contact.

  • Payments

    Fee settlement and balance movements flow through the orchestration and approval path.

  • Analytics

    Strategy performance, assets under management and fee reporting from the same data.
