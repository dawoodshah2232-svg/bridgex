The problem

Partner growth stalls when the numbers are argued about

Introducing brokers bring clients — then spend their energy chasing commission statements. When attribution is unclear and the ledger lives in a spreadsheet, every payout becomes a negotiation.

  1. 01Attribution argumentsTwo partners claim the same client, and there is no rule — or record — to settle it.
  2. 02Spreadsheets as the ledgerCommissions calculated by hand, adjusted by hand, and disputed by hand.
  3. 03Invisible hierarchiesMulti-tier structures tracked in someone's head, so overrides and sub-IB earnings are guesswork.
  4. 04Payouts without proofPartners receive a figure with no statement behind it — and trust erodes a little each month.

Capabilities

From first referral to settled payout

The partner lifecycle is one connected flow: who brought the client, what the program pays, what the ledger says, and what was actually paid.

Partner lifecycle

Registration to active partner, with checks at the gate.

  • Registration, KYC & approval

  • Unique referral links

Programs & models

The commercial terms, expressed as rules the system can run.

  • Rebate programs

  • CPA, volume & performance models

  • Campaign rules

    Time-bound campaigns and bonuses with eligibility conditions and caps.

  • Commissions on open or close

    Earnings recognised at open or close, where the trading feed supports it.

Ledger, payouts & visibility

The money side, with evidence at every step.

  • Commission ledger

  • Clawbacks

    Reversals handled as first-class ledger events, with reason and authorisation.

  • Payout requests & statements

    Partners request payouts and download statements that show the working, not just the total.

  • Partner dashboards & audit

    Referrals, downline and earnings in the partner's own view, over a full audit trail.

How it flows

From referral to settled payout

Six stages, each with a record — so a payout is never a number without a story.

  1. 1

    Register

    The partner registers, completes KYC and is approved; referral links go live.

  2. 2

    Attribute

    Referred clients are attributed by the program's first- or last-touch rule.

  3. 3

    Earn

    Trading activity earns commission under the program's model — CPA, volume or performance.

  4. 4

    Approve

    Ledger entries are reviewed and approved; adjustments and clawbacks are recorded.

  5. 5

    Settle

    Approved earnings settle into a payout, requested by the partner or scheduled.

  6. 6

    Report

    Statements and dashboards show the full trail, from source trade to payout.

Why it matters

A partner channel that scales without the arguments

  • Benefit 1: Attribution that settles disputes

    A configured rule, applied the same way every time, with a log to show it.

  • Benefit 2: A ledger, not a spreadsheet

    Earn, adjust, approve and settle as system states — no more hand-calculated commissions.

  • Benefit 3: Hierarchies you can actually see

    Multi-tier structures mapped and reported, so overrides are computed, not guessed.

  • Benefit 4: Statements partners believe

    Downloadable statements that trace each figure to its source trades.

  • Benefit 5: Clawbacks without chaos

    Reversals as proper ledger events, authorised and visible — not quiet edits.

  • Benefit 6: Partners who self-serve

    Dashboards, links and payout requests in the partner's own portal, cutting support load.

Security & permissions

Controls around partner money

Commissions are financial commitments. Attribution, adjustments and payouts all carry approval and audit.

Control by design

  • Approval on adjustments

    Ledger adjustments and clawbacks require authorisation and carry a reason.

  • Attribution audit trail

    Every attribution decision is logged with the rule applied and the evidence behind it.

  • Payout approval

    Payouts pass approval before release, with the decision recorded.

  • Role-limited partner views

    Partners see their own network and earnings — nothing belonging to another partner.
