The challenges

Many jurisdictions, one client record.

Rules differ by country, ramps differ by region, and referrals outgrow the spreadsheet — unless the system was built jurisdiction-aware.

  1. 01

    One onboarding flow can't fit every jurisdiction

    Rules differ by country and by client type. A single rigid flow either blocks good clients or lets the wrong ones through.

  2. 02

    Fiat on- and off-ramps are a patchwork

    Deposits and withdrawals run through different providers by region, and support can't answer “where is my money” without checking three systems.

  3. 03

    Reviews pile up in a queue nobody owns

    Enhanced reviews, transaction-monitoring flags and document expiries land in a shared inbox — and the oldest cases are the ones nobody sees.

  4. 04

    Referrals grow faster than the records

    Ambassador and affiliate programmes bring in clients faster than the CRM can attribute them, and payouts become a monthly reconstruction.

In practice

Onboard, fund, refer — in any market.

A client from a new market, a deposit across regions, an ambassador paid on time — the same record behind all three.

  1. 01

    A client onboards from a new market

    The journey adapts to the client's country: the right documents, the right checks, the right review queue — and the compliance team sees the case with its full context.

  2. 02

    A deposit is traced end to end

    A fiat deposit is routed to the right provider for the client's region, its status is traced in the CRM, and support answers “where is my money” from the record.

  3. 03

    An ambassador is paid on time

    Referrals are attributed at signup, rewards accrue on the ledger, and the ambassador requests payout from their own dashboard.
