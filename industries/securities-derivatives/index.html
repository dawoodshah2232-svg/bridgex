The challenges

Institutional clients are structures, not records.

When the system can't represent the group, the team carries the structure in their heads — and the risk with it.

  1. One client, many entities

    A group trades through several legal entities with shared contacts and different mandates. A flat client record can't represent that — so teams keep the real structure in their heads.

  2. Authorised persons change; access doesn't

    Signatories and traders join and leave, but permissions lag behind, and nobody can say who could do what, when.

  3. Payments need more than a button

    Institutional funding moves through approvals, mandates and cut-offs. A retail deposit flow can't carry that — and finance ends up reconciling by hand.

  4. Service history is scattered

    Cases, documents and decisions live across email and chat, so a new relationship manager inherits a relationship with no memory.

In practice

The group, the mandate, the money.

Three moments where the structure of the relationship is the work — and where a flat system fails.

  1. 01

    Onboarding a corporate group

    A group with three trading entities is onboarded once: shared documents collected a single time, ownership mapped per entity, and each entity's mandate recorded before trading begins.

  2. 02

    A signatory changes

    When an authorised person leaves, their access is revoked in one place, the change is approved and logged, and the audit trail shows exactly what they could do and until when.

  3. 03

    A funding request with approvals

    A large deposit routes through the approval chain your policy requires, is matched to the right entity, and reconciles against the provider and the platform.
