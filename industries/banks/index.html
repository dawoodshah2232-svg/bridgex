The challenges

Trading should extend the relationship — not split it.

A bank's advantage is trust earned over years. A disconnected trading offer spends that trust; a connected one builds on it.

  1. 01

    Trading feels like a separate business

    A bolt-on platform with its own login and its own brand breaks the relationship the client has with the bank — and doubles the service work.

  2. 02

    Branch and RM teams work blind

    The relationship manager doesn't know a client has started trading; the trading desk doesn't know the client's history with the bank.

  3. 03

    Onboarding asks existing clients to start over

    A client the bank has known for a decade is asked for the same documents again, because the trading journey can't see the banking record.

  4. 04

    Compliance needs evidence, not assurances

    Permissions, approvals and audit trails have to stand up to internal audit — across every channel the client touches.

In practice

The same client, every channel.

Whether the conversation starts at a branch counter or in the app, the record — and the service — is the same.

  1. 01

    An existing client starts trading

    A ten-year account holder opens a trading account from the bank-branded portal. Known details are pre-filled, only the trading-specific checks are new, and the RM sees the journey progress.

  2. 02

    A branch query, answered once

    A client asks at a branch about a trading statement. The branch team sees the same account record as the trading desk — no handoff, no second call.

  3. 03

    A permission change, approved properly

    Raising a client's trading permissions routes through compliance approval, with the decision and the reason recorded for audit.
